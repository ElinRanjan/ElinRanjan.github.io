excerpt: "Symmetry-based structures have advanced molecular spectroscopy and quantum information science, yet connections between these fields remain largely unexplored. We construct a phase space theory of a molecule by jointly treating rotational, vibrational, and nuclear-spin degrees of freedom as the quotient of orientations and normal coordinates by the molecule's point group. The various combinations of how these three degrees of freedom transform under symmetries organize a molecule's states into molecular species, and we show that each species is defined by a particular absolutely maximally entangled (AME) carrier state, or, for 45 species, by a two-dimensional space of them. Up to local unitaries and the order of the sectors, molecular species realize 21 AME states across 75 point groups: a product state, four maximally entangled pairs, and 16 genuinely tripartite states. A Fourier transform on the quotient relates orientation and angular-momentum descriptions while preserving these carrier states. We propose an example experimental protocol for preparing and driving symmetry-adapted bend states of CaOCH$_3$."
